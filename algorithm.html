Who scores tonight

How the picker turns a list of names into a ranked chance of scoring. It estimates how many goals each player should score tonight, then converts that into the odds of at least one.

xG = shots per game × shooting % × opponent defence × home/away
P(goal) = 1 − e−xG

The five steps

1

Shot volume

Shooting is the most stable skill in hockey, so the model starts with how often a player shoots. It blends three seasons, counting each game from this season fully and older games less.

Share of the shot rate each season provides (assumes full 82-game past seasons)

In mid-October a player has about 7 games this season against 49 weighted games from last year, so a hot or cold first week barely moves him. By spring this season dominates.

2

Recent form

Players get promoted to the top line or the power play mid-season. To catch that, the shot rate from the last 10 games gets up to 30% of the say. With fewer than 10 games played it gets proportionally less.

w = 0.30 × (games in sample ÷ 10) shots per game = long-run × (1 − w) + last 10 × w

Only shots are used here, not goals. A run of goals on a few shots is mostly luck. A jump in shots usually means a real change in ice time.

3

Finishing

A raw shooting % from a small sample is unreliable. The model adds 100 shots at the average rate for the player's position before dividing. Small samples get pulled hard toward average. Veterans with 1,000+ shots keep nearly their own rate.

Sh% = (goals + average × 100) ÷ (shots + 100) average = 10.5% forwards, 4.5% defence

4

Matchup

Opponent defence (OppD) compares the goals tonight's opponent allows per game with the league average. Early in the season, 10 games of league-average results are mixed in so one blowout doesn't make a team look terrible. The result is kept between 0.80 and 1.25.

OppD = ((goals allowed + avg × 10) ÷ (games + 10)) ÷ avg home ×1.03 · away ×0.97

Example: a team that has allowed 31 goals in 8 games gives up 3.88 a game, 1.23 times the league's 3.14. After mixing in the 10 average games, OppD is 1.10. That's still a boost, but it no longer takes a rough first week at face value.

5

Probability

Multiplying the pieces gives xG, the goals a player should average in a game like tonight's. Goals arrive at random at roughly a steady rate, which is a Poisson process. Under that model the chance of scoring zero is e−xG, so the chance of at least one is:

P(goal) = 1 − e^(−xG)

That's why 0.58 xG becomes 44%, not 58%. Some of those expected goals come in nights where the player scores twice, and a two-goal night still only wins you one pick.

Warnings it prints

  • missed games

    The player sat out his team's last 2 or more games, so he's probably injured or a healthy scratch. He's moved to the bottom of the group.

  • not playing

    His team has no game on that date. Chance is set to 0%.

  • ambiguous name

    More than one active player has that name (two Elias Petterssons play for Vancouver). It picks the forward. Add a team like (VAN) to be sure.

  • closest match

    The name didn't match exactly, so it used the nearest search result. Check that it's the right player.

What it can't see

  • Tonight's goalie. Starters are confirmed close to game time, and a backup in net helps shooters noticeably.
  • Same-day news. Morning-skate injuries and line changes aren't in the data yet.
  • Linemates and power-play time. These show up only indirectly, through shot volume.
  • Betting odds. Sportsbook "anytime goal scorer" lines bundle all of the above and are the best single check on the model.
